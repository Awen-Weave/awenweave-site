# Tywyn: engagement pack

**Tywyn** (community W04000102), in Gwynedd. Jobs and firms are for **the wider area (W02000026)**, which holds Tywyn, never split to the community. Community census figures are **approximate**. Every figure is marked [S…] (a served claim) or [R…] (a report section), listed at the foot. English text. The Welsh version of this page is to follow.

## The three strongest opportunities

Llefelys's reading of the evidence, by a rule fixed before computing; not a forecast or a recommendation.

1. **Accommodation & food services**: the wider area's highest location quotient (3.39 [S15]) among the sectors whose Gwynedd jobs grew 2015 to 2024 (by 1,000 [R1]†).
2. **Kitchen and catering assistants**: the found skills route with the most Gwynedd adverts (224 [R2]): kitchen assistant into kitchen porter, 10 of 13 essential skills shared.
3. **CPV 45 Construction work**: where most Gwynedd buyers' awards go to suppliers outside Wales (30 awards, April 2022 to March 2026, Sell2Wales [S16]).

## Hard-to-fill posts

Gwynedd's online job adverts (adverts, not vacancies), most in: Professional occupations 1,007; Elementary occupations 832; Associate professional occupations 793 [S17]. Employers saying they have a hard-to-fill vacancy (Employer Skills Survey 2024): Wales 9%, north Wales 9%; share of vacancies hard to fill: Wales 51%, north Wales 49% [R3]. ## Questions to put

- **To the council:** Accommodation & food services is more concentrated in the wider area than in Wales (quotient 3.39 [S15]) and grew in Gwynedd [R1]: what in the council's plans helps it keep growing here?
- **To the council:** 30 awards for CPV 45 Construction work went to suppliers outside Wales (April 2022 to March 2026, Sell2Wales) [S16]: which could local firms bid for, and what would help them?
- **To the North Wales Regional Skills Partnership:** Kitchen and catering assistants had the most Gwynedd adverts (224 [R2]) and a short route into it exists: is training for it offered near Tywyn?
- **To a funder:** skilled trades are the occupations north Wales employers most often find hard to fill (33%; Wales 30% [R3]): would you back local training or apprenticeships in them?
